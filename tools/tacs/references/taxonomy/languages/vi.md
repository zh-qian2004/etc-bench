# vi canonicalization taxonomy

This is the closed language-specific inventory for the language identified by this file's ISO 639-1 code. Language-agnostic numeric and symbol phenomena are defined in `../agnostic.md`.

Each description below is identical to the corresponding definition in the released ETC-Bench. Use it together with the category-level recognition methodology; canonical output forms are defined separately in `../../canonical-contracts/vi.md`.

## orthographic_variants

Category methodology: Recover only genuinely equivalent lexical or character variants and their equivalence group. Do not select a target here.

Semantic value: lexical/character identity and equivalence group.

### `postconsonantal_i_y_variants`

Description: The monophthong /i/ after a consonant may be spelled i or y under different orthographic conventions; traditional spelling tends to retain y, while reform-oriented spelling favors i. Examples: lí/lý, kĩ/kỹ, mĩ/mỹ.

### `qu_i_y_variants`

Description: After qu, the letter representing the /i/ rhyme nucleus may be written i or y, resulting in two coexisting spellings. Examples: quí/quý, quỉ/quỷ, quĩ/quỹ.

### `oa_tone_mark_position_variants`

Description: In open oa syllables, the tone mark may traditionally be placed on either o or a. Examples: hòa/hoà, tỏa/toả, họa/hoạ.

### `oe_tone_mark_position_variants`

Description: In open oe syllables, the tone mark may be placed on either o or e. Examples: khỏe/khoẻ, xòe/xoè, lóe/loé.

### `uy_tone_mark_position_variants`

Description: In open uy syllables, two spelling traditions place the tone mark on either u or y. Examples: thủy/thuỷ, tùy/tuỳ, hủy/huỷ.

### `d_gi_lexical_spelling_doublets`

Description: A small number of native words have dictionary-recognized d/gi spelling doublets because d and gi have merged in northern pronunciation. Examples: dông/giông, dẫm/giẫm, dúi/giúi.

### `loanword_hyphen_space_variants`

Description: Multisyllabic loanwords that have been phoneticized into Vietnamese syllables may be written with hyphens or with ordinary spaces. Examples: ô-tô/ô tô, sô-cô-la/sô cô la, mít-tinh/mít tinh.

### `loanword_hyphen_solid_variants`

Description: Some technological, measurement, and international loanwords may be written with hyphens between syllables or as a single solid word. Examples: ki-lô-gam/kilôgam, mi-li-mét/milimét, đê-xi-mét/đêximét.

### `international_vs_vietnamized_loan_spelling`

Description: Some modern loanwords may retain their international spelling or use a Vietnamese phonetic transcription. Examples: radio/ra-đi-ô, radar/ra-đa, laser/la-de.

## homophonic_substitutions

Category methodology: Recover only the stated equivalence relation and applicability. Do not collapse arbitrary homophones or select a representative here.

Semantic value: pronunciation group and contextually intended lexical item.

### `i_y_orthographic_variants`

Description: In some Vietnamese syllables, i/y are historically and normatively coexisting spelling variants; they generally do not cause a pronunciation difference and may occur in given names, surnames, or forms of address. Examples: Lý/Lí, Mỹ/Mĩ, Tý/Tí.

### `tr_ch_regional_merger`

Description: In many Southern Vietnamese accents, the initials tr and ch may merge or become very similar, so differently spelled personal names cannot be distinguished from speech alone. Examples: Trinh/Chinh, Trung/Chung, Trúc/Chúc.

### `s_x_regional_merger`

Description: In some Vietnamese dialects, especially Southern and certain Central varieties, the contrast between the initials s and x is weakened or neutralized, causing differently spelled words or names to become homophonous. Examples: Sương/Xương, Sinh/Xinh.

### `foreign_name_vietnamese_reading_variants`

Description: Foreign surnames or given names in Vietnamese texts are often pronounced according to Vietnamese phonology, and the same pronunciation may correspond to different Latin-letter spellings. Examples: Li/Lý, Lee/Li, Chen/Chân (Vietnamese-style readings only).
