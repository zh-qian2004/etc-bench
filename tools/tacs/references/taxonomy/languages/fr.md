# fr canonicalization taxonomy

This is the closed language-specific inventory for the language identified by this file's ISO 639-1 code. Language-agnostic numeric and symbol phenomena are defined in `../agnostic.md`.

Each description below is identical to the corresponding definition in the released ETC-Bench. Use it together with the category-level recognition methodology; canonical output forms are defined separately in `../../canonical-contracts/fr.md`.

## abbreviations

Category methodology: Recover the contextually intended expansion and grammatical realization. This does not authorize rendering without an exact contract.

Semantic value: intended expansion and grammatical realization.

### `fixed_phrase_abbreviations`

Description: Frequently used connectives and explanatory phrases may be written using conventional abbreviations, e.g., etc. → et cetera, c.-à-d. → c’est-à-dire, p. ex. → par exemple.

## orthographic_variants

Category methodology: Recover only genuinely equivalent lexical or character variants and their equivalence group. Do not select a target here.

Semantic value: lexical/character identity and equivalence group.

### `circumflex_i_u_reform`

Description: The 1990 spelling rectifications allow the circumflex to be omitted from most instances of i and u, so traditional and rectified spellings coexist, e.g., coût/cout, paraître/paraitre, entraîner/entrainer.

### `grave_accent_harmonization`

Description: The 1990 rectifications extend the use of è before a syllable containing a mute e, resulting in traditional and rectified spellings such as événement/évènement, réglementaire/règlementaire, je céderai/je cèderai.

### `eler_eter_consonant_reform`

Description: In the conjugation of some verbs ending in -eler or -eter, traditional spelling doubles the consonant, whereas the 1990 rectifications use è followed by a single consonant, e.g., j’amoncelle/j’amoncèle, tu époussettes/tu époussètes, il ruisselle/il ruissèle.

### `diaeresis_repositioning`

Description: The 1990 rectifications may move the diaeresis to the vowel that is actually pronounced or add one to indicate that a vowel is pronounced separately, producing spelling pairs such as aiguë/aigüe, ambiguë/ambigüe, gageure/gageüre.

### `compound_solidification`

Description: Under the 1990 rectifications, some compounds may be written as single words instead of with hyphens, while the traditional forms remain valid, e.g., week-end/weekend, porte-monnaie/portemonnaie, tic-tac/tictac.

### `compound_plural_regularization`

Description: The 1990 rectifications regularize singular and plural marking in some verb–noun or preposition–noun compounds, so traditional and rectified forms may coexist, e.g., des après-midi/des après-midis, un compte-gouttes/un compte-goutte, des pèse-lettre/des pèse-lettres.

### `loanword_plural_variants`

Description: Loanword plurals may retain forms from the source language or older usage, or take the French plural ending -s, producing variants such as matchs/matches, maximums/maxima, sandwichs/sandwiches.

### `loanword_diacritic_adaptation`

Description: Some loanwords may retain their original unaccented spelling or take diacritics in accordance with French pronunciation and accentuation rules, e.g., diesel/diésel, revolver/révolver, allegro/allégro.

### `hyphenated_number_reform`

Description: The 1990 rectifications allow systematic hyphenation between all components of compound numerals, so older spaced forms coexist with rectified forms, e.g., vingt et un/vingt-et-un, cent trois/cent-trois, deux mille cinq cents/deux-mille-cinq-cents.

### `ayer_i_y_conjugation_variants`

Description: For most verbs ending in -ayer, the stem y may be retained or replaced with i in certain conjugated forms, resulting in two accepted spellings, e.g., je paie/je paye, j’essaie/j’essaye, il balaie/il balaye.

### `established_lexical_doublets`

Description: French also has lexical spelling doublets that do not result from a single modern reform rule but have long been jointly recognized by dictionaries, e.g., clé/clef, cuiller/cuillère, lis/lys.

## homophonic_substitutions

Category methodology: Recover only the stated equivalence relation and applicability. Do not collapse arbitrary homophones or select a representative here.

Semantic value: pronunciation group and contextually intended lexical item.

### `proper_name_common_word_homophones`

Description: A proper name and a common word may be homophones, and the capitalization and semantic reference of the proper name cannot be determined from speech alone; for example, the first name Jean is homophonous with gens, Claire with the adjective clair, and Maud with mot.

### `proper_name_proper_name_homophones`

Description: Names of different people or entities may be pronounced identically in French while differing in spelling and reference; for example, Renaud and Renault, or Anne and Ann.

### `proper_name_graphemic_spelling_variants`

Description: The same or different proper names may have the same French pronunciation despite spelling variations caused by international forms or letter substitutions, e.g., Catherine/Katherine, Sofia/Sophia, Kevin/Kévin.

### `diacritic_omission_homophones`

Description: When accents or other French diacritics are omitted, the resulting written form may be pronounced the same as the standard form, e.g., Éric/Eric, où/ou, là/la.
