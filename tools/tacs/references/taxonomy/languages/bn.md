# bn canonicalization taxonomy

This is the closed language-specific inventory for the language identified by this file's ISO 639-1 code. Language-agnostic numeric and symbol phenomena are defined in `../agnostic.md`.

Each description below is identical to the corresponding definition in the released ETC-Bench. Use it together with the category-level recognition methodology; canonical output forms are defined separately in `../../canonical-contracts/bn.md`.

## orthographic_variants

Category methodology: Recover only genuinely equivalent lexical or character variants and their equivalence group. Do not select a target here.

Semantic value: lexical/character identity and equivalence group.

### `nukta_flap_variants`

Description: The retroflex flap letters ড় and ঢ় are sometimes written as decomposed forms with nukta, ড় and ঢ়, representing the same /ɽ/ and /ɽʱ/ sounds. For example: বড়/বড়, পড়া/পড়া, আড়ি/আড়ি.

### `historical_i_length_variants`

Description: In some native words, loanwords, and personal names, parallel spellings occur with the short vowel ই and the historically long vowel ঈ. For example: বাড়ি/বাড়ী, শ্রেণি/শ্রেণী, and মিতালি/মিতালী.

### `historical_u_length_variants`

Description: In some words and personal names, parallel spellings may occur with the short vowel উ and the historically long vowel ঊ. For example: রুপা/রূপা, রুপালি/রূপালী, and দুর্গাপূজা/দুর্গাপুজা.

### `khanda_ta_variants`

Description: In some words derived from Sanskrit, Khanda Ta (ৎ) and the explicit ত্ can form traditional and modern or alternative spellings. For example: উৎসব/উত্সব, সৎকার/সত্কার, and জগৎ/জগত্.

### `loanword_vowel_adaptation`

Description: Loanwords from Arabic, Persian, English, and other languages often have parallel spellings in Bengali because of differences in local pronunciation and transliteration conventions. For example: কোরআন/কুরআন, কোরবানি/কুরবানি, and ফেব্রুয়ারি/ফেব্রুয়ারি.

## homophonic_substitutions

Category methodology: Recover only the stated equivalence relation and applicability. Do not collapse arbitrary homophones or select a representative here.

Semantic value: pronunciation group and contextually intended lexical item.

### `i_ii_name_variants`

Description: In modern bn pronunciation, the spelling distinction in length between ই and ঈ is generally not audible, especially in personal names. For example: মিনা/মীনা, শিলা/শীলা, and রহিম/রহীম.

### `u_uu_name_variants`

Description: In modern bn, the historical length distinction between উ and ঊ is generally not retained in pronunciation; therefore, differently spelled personal names may be pronounced identically. For example: পূজা/পুজা, রূপা/রুপা, and সুরভি/সূরভি.

### `n_nukta_name_variants`

Description: In the spelling of Bengali personal names, the distinction between ন and ণ is usually lost in modern bn pronunciation; therefore, the same name may appear in two spellings. For example: অরুণ/অরুন, কিরণ/কিরন, and বরুণ/বরুন.

### `sha_ssha_name_variants`

Description: In Bengali pronunciation, শ and ষ are generally pronounced as the same postalveolar or palatal fricative; therefore, the শ/ষ spelling distinction may create homophonous personal names. For example: সুশমা/সুষমা, সুশ্মিতা/সুষ্মিতা, and রোশন/রোষণ.

### `ja_ya_name_variants`

Description: In bn, য and জ merge into the same /জ/ pronunciation in word-initial position in many names, so the same personal name may have common alternative spellings. For example: জাহিদ/যাহিদ, জাকির/যাকির, and জাকিয়া/যাকিয়া.

### `ri_ri_vowel_name_variants`

Description: The pronunciation of the ঋ-kar in modern Bengali is generally close to রি, so names containing ঋ may be homophonous with names spelled with রি. For example: ঋতু/রিতু, ঋষি/রিশি, and ঋদ্ধি/রিদ্ধি.

### `ai_aae_name_variants`

Description: In personal names of Arabic-Persian origin, the spelling sequences আই and আঈ/াঈ often merge in bn into the same diphthong or long 'আই' pronunciation. For example: সাইদ/সাঈদ, নাইম/নাঈম, and রাইস/রাঈস.

### `abdul_cluster_spelling_variants`

Description: In Bengali transliterations of Arabic names, both আবদ- and আব্দ- are common spellings; in spoken bn, the form with the added d consonant and the form written with দ are generally pronounced identically. For example: আবদুল/আব্দুল, আবদুর/আব্দুর, and আবদুস/আব্দুস.

### `orthographic_y_glide_name_variants`

Description: In some personal names, Bengali spellings with য় and common spellings with য are pronounced with the same /য়/ or /j/ sound; this mainly reflects differences in name-spelling conventions. For example: বিজয়/বিজয়, মায়া/মায়া, and জয়ন্ত/জয়ন্ত.
